02 Research

Multi-Decadal Land-Cover Mapping of the Chittagong Hill Tracts Using Machine Learning and Spatial Validation

Submitted to ICCIT 2026 (double-blind peer review) — currently under review

  • Originated as B.Sc. thesis (2024, graded A+) on machine-learning classification of deforestation from Landsat satellite imagery, then independently extended in 2026 into a full conference paper without institutional funding or supervision.
  • Rebuilt the entire processing pipeline with free and open-source tools (Google Earth Engine, Python, QGIS) for full reproducibility at no cost.
  • Compared three classifiers under spatially-blocked holdout validation: Support Vector Machine (78.04%), Random Forest (77.10%), CART (72.90%); benchmarked against the Hansen Global Forest Change dataset.
  • Reported area-adjusted 2023 classification accuracy of 73.17% (95% CI: 64.34–82.00%), with a forest-definition sensitivity analysis to test robustness.
  • Key finding: combined forest area increased 4.4% (+20,693 ha) between 1993 and 2023, while Dense Forest area declined by 8,235 ha — a more nuanced outcome than a single headline figure.
  • Code and data pipeline released publicly on GitHub for reproducibility.

Energy-Aware Efficiency of Lightweight Vision Models Under Post-Training Compression

Research proposal — Proxy metrics, measurement reliability, and cross-platform behaviour in Green AI

  • Proposes a controlled, hardware-instrumented study testing whether FLOPs reduction from quantization and pruning produces proportional reductions in measured energy, across three lightweight vision architectures (ResNet-18, MobileNetV3-Small, EfficientNet-B0) and six compression states.
  • Validates the software estimator CodeCarbon against hardware power counters specifically for compressed models, where existing validation studies do not cover this case.
  • Tests whether the rank ordering of compression methods by measured energy efficiency is preserved across x86 and ARM platforms.
  • Motivated by a finding from the applicant's own thesis: validation-method choice (not algorithm choice) reversed the headline conclusion from forest loss to broadly stable — the same scepticism is applied here to unvalidated efficiency proxies.

Research interests: data science, sustainable & green AI, environmental machine learning, model compression, and efficient data-driven approaches to large-scale monitoring.
